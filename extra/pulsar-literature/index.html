Coherent radiation
LAE (linear acceleration emission)
Charges accelerated parallel to the magnetic field radiate without transverse gyration. Cocke proposed stimulated LAE as a pulsar radio mechanism, and Melrose developed the amplified maser theory and its spectral consequences.
Cocke (1973), Melrose (1978)
Beam geometry
Original patchy pulsar radio-beam model
Profile and polarization statistics showed that emission components are often distributed irregularly within an approximately circular beam boundary. This established the patchy-beam alternative to geometrically ordered nested cones.
Lyne & Manchester (1988)
Beam geometry
Circulating-subbeam carousel in PSR B0943+10
Cartographic reconstruction of drifting subpulses identified 20 subbeams circulating around the magnetic axis in approximately 37 rotation periods. It provided a direct empirical bridge between organized polar-cap structure and conal average profiles.
Deshpande & Rankin (1999), Deshpande & Rankin (2001)
Magnetospheric Propagation Effects
Induced scattering in pulsar magnetospheres
Induced scattering can convert trapped subluminal plasma waves into escaping modes and redistribute an intense radio beam in frequency and direction. The mechanism links plasma-wave generation to observable luminosity, low-frequency turnovers and pulse morphology.
Lyubarskii (1996), Lyubarskii & Petrova (1996)
Coherent radiation
RPE (relativistic plasma emission)
Beam-driven instabilities in a relativistically streaming electron-positron plasma excite longitudinal or ordinary-mode waves that may convert into escaping radio emission. Detailed dispersion calculations also exposed stringent constraints on wave growth and escape.
Melrose & Gedalin (1999), Gedalin et al. (2002)
Coherent radiation
ADE (anomalous Doppler emission) and the cyclotron-Cherenkov maser
Early cyclotron-instability work developed into a direct radio-emission model in which anomalous cyclotron-Cherenkov and Cherenkov-drift resonances amplify escaping electromagnetic modes in the outer magnetosphere.
Machabeli & Usov (1979), Kazbegi et al. (1991), Lyutikov, Blandford & Machabeli (1999)
Coherent radiation
FEM (free-electron maser) and laser models
Relativistic particles interacting with self-generated longitudinal turbulence or a transverse electromagnetic wiggler bunch coherently and radiate intense short radio bursts. These models adapt the free-electron-laser mechanism to pulsar plasma conditions.
Schopper et al. (2003), Fung & Kuijpers (2004)
Inner acceleration
PSG (partially screened gap) model
Thermionic ion flow partially screens the polar-cap accelerating field, reducing the spark drift rate relative to a pure vacuum gap. The model connects drifting subpulses with polar-cap heating and strong non-dipolar surface fields.
Gil, Melikidze & Geppert (2003)
Beam geometry
Multi-altitude pulsar radio-beam model
Radio emission at one observing frequency occupies a range of altitudes near the last open field lines. An age-dependent altitude distribution and number of active patches reproduce diverse profile morphologies without requiring fixed nested cones.
Karastergiou & Johnston (2007)
Beam geometry
Fan-beam model for radio pulsars
Broadband coherent emission along active magnetic flux tubes forms radially extended subbeams rather than nested rings. The model yields distinct pulse-width, impact-angle and intensity relations that reproduce observed profile statistics.
Wang et al. (2014)
Beam geometry
Stream-based fan-beam model
Azimuthally narrow plasma streams generate fan-shaped beams whose sightline cuts reproduce apparent radius-to-frequency mapping, component-centroid shifts and complex profiles without separate core and nested-cone emission zones.
Dyks & Rudak (2015)
Beam geometry
Flaring-spiral radio-beam geometry
A plasma stream following a flaring helix can project as both cone-like rings and radial fan beams. The geometry explains apparently nested profile components and unusually broad bifurcated features without physical nested cones.
Dyks (2017)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Cold-plasma dispersion and DM (dispersion measure)
Frequency-dependent pulse arrival times established propagation through ionized plasma. The DM is the line-of-sight integral of free-electron density, DM = ∫ ne dl, and produces an arrival-time delay approximately proportional to ν-2. This made pulsars direct probes of Galactic free electrons.
Hewish et al. (1968)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Interstellar Faraday rotation of pulsar radio emission
Faraday rotation of linearly polarized pulsar emission provided an RM (rotation measure), the line-of-sight integral of electron density weighted by magnetic field. Combining RM with DM constrains the mean line-of-sight Galactic magnetic field and keeps interstellar Faraday rotation distinct from generalized Faraday rotation inside a pulsar magnetosphere.
Smith (1968)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Thin-screen and distributed-medium pulse broadening
Multipath propagation gives rays different geometric delays and broadens an impulse into a PBF (pulse-broadening function). Williamson derived distinct response functions for a localized thin screen and for scattering distributed along a substantial part of the path, establishing that an exponential tail is geometry-dependent rather than universal.
Williamson (1974)
Magnetospheric Propagation Effects
Normal modes in strongly magnetized pulsar pair plasma
Dispersion relations and polarization properties for ordinary, extraordinary and Alfvén-like modes established a physical basis for birefringence, refraction and mode-dependent escape in a relativistic electron-positron magnetosphere.
Arons & Barnard (1986), Barnard & Arons (1986)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Refractive and diffractive interstellar scattering
Small-scale stochastic irregularities generate DISS (diffractive interstellar scintillation), characterized by decorrelation bandwidth and timescale, while larger-scale gradients produce RISS (refractive interstellar scintillation), image wander and slow flux modulation. Refractive structure also modulates diffractive observables; the two regimes do not require separate media.
Cordes, Pidwerbetsky & Lovelace (1986), Romani, Narayan & Blandford (1986)
Interstellar Medium and GalaxyDiscovery
ESEs (extreme scattering events)
Observation: Compact radio sources showed dramatic, strongly chromatic flux excursions unlike ordinary stochastic scintillation. Interpretation: Fiedler and collaborators attributed them to refractive focusing and defocusing by discrete ionized structures. Later significance: Clouds, filaments, current sheets and plasma-lens caustics remain competing physical descriptions.
Fiedler et al. (1987)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Refractive modulation in pulsar dynamic spectra
Long-term observations showed that large-scale refraction changes flux density, DISS bandwidth and scintillation timescale and creates drifting bands in the dynamic spectrum I(t, ν). The time-frequency interference pattern therefore records evolving propagation paths through the ISM.
Gupta, Rickett & Lyne (1994)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Finite and truncated scattering screens
Interpretation: Spatially confined disks, filaments and screens can truncate the scattered image, modify the PBF (pulse-broadening function), remove flux and flatten the usual frequency scaling of angular and temporal broadening. Later significance: These signatures distinguish finite transverse geometry from merely changing the turbulent spectral index.
Cordes & Lazio (2001)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Theory of parabolic scintillation arcs
Interpretation: Differential geometric delays and Doppler frequencies of interfering paths share a dependence on scattering angle and generate parabolas in delay-Doppler space. Walker and collaborators showed that strong anisotropy helps produce arclets; Cordes and collaborators mapped secondary-spectrum power to the scattered image and treated inner scale, finite source size and multiple screens. Later significance: Arc curvature constrains geometry only jointly with effective velocity and anisotropy orientation.
Walker et al. (2004), Cordes et al. (2006)
Magnetospheric Propagation Effects
Polarization transfer, mode coupling and cyclotron absorption
Propagation through an inhomogeneous relativistic pair plasma changes the natural modes through polarization limiting, mode coupling and cyclotron resonance. The calculation showed how initially orthogonal modes can emerge elliptical and non-orthogonal, linking magnetospheric transfer to observed circular polarization.
Petrova (2006)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Pulsar RMs (rotation measures) and Galactic magnetic-field structure
A large pulsar RM sample combined RM, DM and distance information to map electron-weighted line-of-sight magnetic fields in three dimensions and infer large-scale field directions and reversals associated with Galactic spiral structure.
Han et al. (2006)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Interstellar holography
Treating a pulsar dynamic spectrum as an in-line interference hologram enabled reconstruction of the complex scattered electric field in delay-Doppler space. Applied to PSR B0834+06, the method exposed highly structured, strongly anisotropic paths and quantified their multipath timing delay, going beyond a power-only secondary spectrum.
Walker et al. (2008)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Direct imaging of highly anisotropic interstellar scattering
Observation: VLBI (very long baseline interferometry) combined with secondary-spectrum analysis mapped the scattered image of PSR B0834+06 at 100-microarcsecond resolution, revealing narrow, nearly one-dimensional structures and a second offset component. Later significance: The result made sheet- and filament-like interpretations testable but did not by itself select one physical origin.
Brisken et al. (2010)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Convergent plasma-sheet lens model
Interpretation: Underdense sheets viewed close to edge-on were proposed as convergent refractive lenses capable of producing multiple images, ESEs (extreme scattering events) and parabolic arcs without requiring overpressured compact clouds. Later significance: The model established that both overdense divergent and underdense convergent plasma geometries must be considered.
Pen & King (2012)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Corrugated plasma-sheet model
Interpretation: Weakly corrugated current sheets nearly aligned with the line of sight were proposed to generate fold caustics and strings of refracted images, reproducing highly anisotropic scintillation and reverse arclets. Later significance: This is a testable physical interpretation of discrete scattering structures, not an established universal ISM geometry.
Pen & Levin (2014)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Frequency-dependent effective DM (dispersion measure)
Multipath propagation samples different electron columns at different frequencies, so the effective DM can be chromatic and arrival-time perturbations need not follow one exact ν-2 law across a wide band. The effect introduces correlated chromatic noise and a potential bias in precision pulsar timing.
Cordes, Shannon & Stinebring (2016)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Refractive propagation corrections for precision pulsar timing
Wideband observations and stochastic models separated dispersive, scattering and refractive geometric delays and demonstrated mitigation of chromatic propagation perturbations. This moved ISM modelling from a distance-estimation correction to a core noise treatment for PTA (pulsar timing array) analysis.
Coles et al. (2015), Shannon and Cordes (2017)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Reconstructing multiple interstellar scattering screens
Combining VLBI visibilities with single-station intensity correlations localized both members of interfering image pairs and separated contributions from complex, multiple-screen geometries. The method removed the assumption that one dominant thin screen must explain every secondary spectrum.
Simard et al. (2019)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Double-lens scintillometry
Observation: The secondary spectrum of PSR B1508+55 changed through annual velocity modulation, line-of-sight motion and screen-screen interference. Interpretation: A two-screen model reconstructed the relative geometry and motion. Later significance: Scintillometry became an astrometric and tomographic technique rather than only a description of propagation noise.
Sprenger et al. (2022)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Pulsar scintillation through thick and thin
Observation: Sensitive secondary spectra revealed multiple arc curvatures and a mixture of thin, diffuse, filled and truncated structures along individual sightlines. Interpretation: Candidate screens include the diffuse ISM, H II regions, supernova remnants, bubbles and pulsar bow shocks. Later significance: The survey demonstrated that one dominant thin screen, several thin screens and distributed material must all remain viable on different sightlines.
Ocker et al. (2024)
Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
Cusp-catastrophe plasma-lens model
Interpretation: A cusp profile described by an A3 catastrophe was proposed for ESEs (extreme scattering events) and double-lensing features, extending the A2 fold-caustic description of corrugated sheets. Later significance: It supplies a compact universal lens description to test, not evidence that every plasma lens is a cusp.
Jow, Pen & Baker (2024)
