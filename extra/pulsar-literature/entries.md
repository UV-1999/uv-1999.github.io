## Chandrasekhar limit

**Year:** 1929, 1931, 1935
**Topic:** Neutron star structure and fundamental physics
**Type:**

Electron degeneracy cannot support a cold, non-rotating white dwarf above approximately 1.4 solar masses. The limit establishes why sufficiently massive degenerate stellar cores must collapse toward a neutron star or black hole.

**References:**
- [Anderson (1929)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1929ZPhy...56..851A/abstract>)
- [Chandrasekhar (1931a)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1931MNRAS..91..456C/abstract>)
- [Chandrasekhar (1931b)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1931ApJ....74...81C/abstract>)
- [Chandrasekhar (1935)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1935MNRAS..95..207C/abstract>)

## Discovery of the neutron

**Year:** 1932
**Topic:** Neutron star structure and fundamental physics
**Type:**

Chadwick established the neutron as a fundamental particle. This made neutron stars physically plausible and provided the constituent particle implied by their name.

## Birth of radio astronomy

**Year:** 1933
**Topic:** Emission and magnetospheric physics
**Type:** Discovery

Jansky identified extraterrestrial radio emission from the direction of the Galactic centre. This opened the observing domain in which pulsars would later be discovered.

**References:**
- [Jansky (1933)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1933PIRE...21.1387J/abstract>)

## Neutron stars proposed as products of supernovae

**Year:** 1934
**Topic:** Population and evolution
**Type:**

Baade and Zwicky explicitly proposed neutron stars as compact remnants produced by supernova explosions. This was the first direct astrophysical prediction of the objects later identified observationally as pulsars.

**References:**
- [Baade &amp; Zwicky (1934)](<https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.20.5.254>)

## Neutron-star structure and the TOV (Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff) equation

**Year:** 1939, 2001, 2013
**Topic:** Neutron star structure and fundamental physics
**Type:**

Oppenheimer and Volkoff derived hydrostatic equilibrium for a relativistic star made of neutron matter. The resulting TOV framework remains the standard basis of neutron-star mass-radius calculations.

**References:**

## Alfvén waves and MHD (magnetohydrodynamics)

**Year:** 1942
**Topic:** Emission and magnetospheric physics
**Type:**

Alfvén introduced waves carried by magnetic tension in conducting plasma. MHD and Alfvénic disturbances are fundamental to pulsar magnetospheres, winds, current systems and nebulae.

**References:**
- [Alfvén (1942)](<https://doi.org/10.1038/150405d0>)

## Microwave radiometer and radiometer sensitivity

**Year:** 1946
**Topic:**
**Type:** Instrumentation

Dicke developed switched microwave radiometry and quantified receiver-noise sensitivity. The radiometer equation remains basic to pulsar detectability, telescope sensitivity and observing-time estimates.

**References:**
- [Dicke (1946)](<https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1770483>)

## Radio interferometry

**Year:** 1946
**Topic:**

Ryle and Vonberg demonstrated astronomical radio interferometry. Its descendants provide pulsar positions, proper motions, parallaxes, scattering images and associations with nebulae or binaries.

**Year:** 1951

Radio detection of the neutral-hydrogen line enabled mapping of Galactic gas and kinematics. H I absorption and emission remain useful for constraining pulsar distances and line-of-sight environments.

## Shapiro delay

**Year:** 1964, 1986
**Topic:** Timing and dynamics
**Type:**

A signal acquires an additional propagation delay while traversing a companion's gravitational potential. In binary-pulsar timing, its range and shape constrain the companion mass and orbital inclination, enabling strong-field tests of gravity.

**References:**
- [Shapiro (1964)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1964PhRvL..13..789S/abstract>)
- [Backer &amp; Hellings (1986)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1986ARA%26A..24..537B/abstract>)

## Interplanetary scintillation of compact radio sources

**Year:** 1964
**Topic:**
**Type:** Discovery

Hewish, Scott and Wills established interplanetary scintillation as a probe of compact radio sources. The Cambridge scintillation survey and its chart records directly created the observational route to pulsar discovery.

**References:**
- [Hewish et al. (1964)](<https://doi.org/10.1038/2031214a0>)

## RM (rotation measure) synthesis

**Year:** 1966, 2005, 2009
**Topic:** Polarisation and magnetic geometry
**Type:**

Reconstructs polarized emission as a function of Faraday depth from complex polarization across wavelength squared. It resolves multiple magneto-ionic components and avoids the ambiguities that limit straight PA (position-angle) fits.

**References:**
- [Burn (1966)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1966MNRAS.133...67B/abstract>)
- [Brentjens &amp; de Bruyn (2005)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005A%26A...441.1217B/abstract>)
- [Heald (2009)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009IAUS..259..591H/abstract>)

## Rotating magnetized neutron star as an energy source

**Year:** 1967
**Topic:** Emission and magnetospheric physics
**Type:**

Pacini proposed that a rapidly rotating, strongly magnetized neutron star could power the Crab Nebula. This was the key theoretical precursor immediately before pulsars were recognized.

**References:**
- [Pacini (1967)](<https://doi.org/10.1038/216567a0>)

## First pulsar discovered

**Year:** 1967, 1968, 1974
**Topic:** Population and evolution
**Type:** Discovery

Jocelyn Bell Burnell identified the first regularly pulsing celestial radio source in Cambridge interplanetary-scintillation records in August 1967. The discovery paper was published in 1968 and established the observational existence of pulsars. In 1974, Antony Hewish received half of the Nobel Prize in Physics for his decisive role in the discovery of pulsars.

**References:**
- [Hewish et al. (1968)](<https://doi.org/10.1038/217709a0>)
- [Nobel Prize in Physics (1974)](<https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1974/summary/>)

## Thin-screen approximation for interstellar scattering

**Year:** 1968
**Topic:** Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
**Type:**

Scheuer applied a thin phase-changing screen model to pulsar intensity variations, showing how small-scale electron-density irregularities can produce interstellar scintillation. Thin-screen geometry subsequently became a standard approximation for pulsar scattering, but it should not be presented as a universal physical configuration.

**References:**
- [Scheuer (1968)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1968Natur.218..920S/abstract>)

## Pulsars identified as rotating neutron stars

**Year:** 1968
**Topic:** Neutron star structure and fundamental physics
**Type:**

Gold connected the newly discovered pulsating radio sources to rotating neutron stars in May 1968. Pacini then explicitly connected pulsars with his earlier rotating magnetized neutron-star model in July 1968.

**References:**
- [Gold (1968)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1968Natur.218..731G/abstract>)

## Discovery and identification of the Crab pulsar

**Year:** 1968, 1969
**Topic:** Emission and magnetospheric physics
**Type:** Discovery

<b>Observation:</b> Staelin and Reifenstein first reported the variable radio source NP 0532 near the Crab Nebula without detecting a period. <b>Later significance:</b> Lovelace and collaborators, followed by Comella and collaborators, established its approximately 33-millisecond periodicity and identification with the Crab Nebula, firmly linking a pulsar to a supernova remnant.

**References:**
- [Staelin &amp; Reifenstein (1968)](<https://doi.org/10.1126/science.162.3861.1481>)
- [Lovelace et al. (1968)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1968IAUC.2113....1L/abstract>)
- [Comella et al. (1969)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1969Natur.221..453C/abstract>)

## Cold-plasma dispersion and DM (dispersion measure)

**Year:** 1968
**Topic:** Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
**Type:**

Frequency-dependent pulse arrival times established propagation through ionized plasma. The DM is the line-of-sight integral of free-electron density, <span class="math-symbol">DM = &#8747; n<sub>e</sub> dl</span>, and produces an arrival-time delay approximately proportional to <span class="math-symbol">&#957;<sup>-2</sup></span>. This made pulsars direct probes of Galactic free electrons.

**References:**
- [Hewish et al. (1968)](<https://doi.org/10.1038/217709a0>)

## Interstellar Faraday rotation of pulsar radio emission

**Year:** 1968
**Topic:** Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
**Type:**

Faraday rotation of linearly polarized pulsar emission provided an RM (rotation measure), the line-of-sight integral of electron density weighted by magnetic field. Combining RM with DM constrains the mean line-of-sight Galactic magnetic field and keeps interstellar Faraday rotation distinct from generalized Faraday rotation inside a pulsar magnetosphere.

**References:**
- [Smith (1968)](<https://doi.org/10.1038/220891a0>)

## FFA (fast folding algorithm)

**Year:** 1969, 2009, 2017
**Topic:** Population and evolution
**Type:** Search

The FFA folds a time series over a dense, efficiently organized grid of trial periods and evaluates the resulting profiles coherently. It is especially sensitive to long-period, narrow-duty-cycle pulsars whose harmonics are poorly recovered by standard Fourier searches.

**References:**
- [Staelin (1969)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1969IEEEP..57..724S/abstract>)
- [Kondratiev et al. (2009)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...702..692K/abstract>)
- [Cameron et al. (2017)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.468.1994C/abstract>)

## Goldreich-Julian pulsar electrodynamics

**Year:** 1969
**Topic:** Emission and magnetospheric physics
**Type:**

Goldreich and Julian showed that a rotating magnetized neutron star requires a charge-filled, approximately corotating magnetosphere with a characteristic charge density. Their model distinguished open and closed magnetic-field regions and associated charge outflow from the polar caps.

**References:**
- [Goldreich &amp; Julian (1969)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1969ApJ...157..869G/abstract>)

## First observed pulsar glitch

**Year:** 1969
**Topic:** Timing and dynamics
**Type:** Discovery

The Vela pulsar underwent an abrupt fractional decrease in its rotation period of approximately two parts per million between 24 February and 3 March 1969, revealing the first observed pulsar glitch. An independent detection by Reichley and Downs was published in the same issue of <em>Nature</em>, making the observational priority effectively simultaneous.

**References:**
- [Radhakrishnan &amp; Manchester (1969)](<https://doi.org/10.1038/222228a0>)
- [Reichley &amp; Downs (1969)](<https://doi.org/10.1038/222229a0>)

## Discovery of optical signals from pulsar NP 0532

**Year:** 1969
**Topic:** Emission and magnetospheric physics
**Type:** Discovery

Optical pulses at the Crab pulsar's radio period provided the first optical identification of a pulsar. The result established that pulsar emission spans widely separated wavebands and tied the compact source directly to the visible supernova remnant.

**References:**
- [Cocke et al. (1969)](<https://doi.org/10.1038/221525a0>)

## Shklovskii effect

**Year:** 1970
**Topic:** Timing and dynamics
**Type:**

A pulsar's transverse motion produces an apparent radial acceleration and therefore a positive contribution proportional to transverse speed squared divided by distance to the observed spin or orbital period derivative. Removing this kinematic term is essential when inferring intrinsic spin-down, characteristic age and binary orbital decay.

**References:**
- [Shklovskii (1970)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1970SvA....13..562S/abstract>)

## Radius-to-frequency mapping

**Year:** 1970, 1978
**Topic:** Beam geometry
**Type:**

Pulse width and the separation of profile components systematically increase at lower observing frequencies. Komesaroff proposed this as a consequence of higher-frequency emission being produced closer to the neutron-star surface than lower-frequency emission; this became known as radius-to-frequency mapping.

**References:**
- [Komesaroff (1970)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1970Natur.225..612K/abstract>)
- [Cordes (1978)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1978ApJ...222.1006C/abstract>)

## Large-Vaughan Molonglo survey

**Year:** 1971
**Topic:** Population and evolution
**Type:** Survey

A Galactic-plane search at Molonglo discovered 31 pulsars, including objects with large dispersion measures. The enlarged distant-pulsar sample helped establish how survey sensitivity and ionized Galactic structure shape the observed population.

**References:**
- [Large &amp; Vaughan (1971)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1971MNRAS.151..277L/abstract>)

## First accretion-powered X-ray pulsar

**Year:** 1971
**Topic:** Emission and magnetospheric physics
**Type:** Discovery

Uhuru observations revealed coherent 4.8-second X-ray pulsations from Cen X-3. Its subsequent binary interpretation established the class of accretion-powered X-ray pulsars, distinct from rotation-powered radio pulsars.

**References:**
- [Giacconi et al. (1971)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1971ApJ...167L..67G/abstract>)

## First reported pulsed high-energy gamma-ray emission from a pulsar

**Year:** 1971
**Topic:** Emission and magnetospheric physics
**Type:** Discovery

<b>Observation:</b> Balloon-borne measurements reported pulsed emission above 50 megaelectronvolts from the Crab pulsar. <b>Later significance:</b> This was the first published high-energy gamma-ray detection claim; later satellite observations established the emission with much greater sensitivity and certainty.

**References:**
- [Browning, Ramsden and Wright (1971)](<https://doi.org/10.1038/physci232099a0>)

## A model of pulsars

**Year:** 1971
**Topic:** Emission and magnetospheric physics
**Type:**

Sturrock developed an early polar-cap cascade picture in which accelerated particles emit energetic photons that create electron-positron pairs. The work helped establish pair production as the source of magnetospheric plasma required by pulsar emission models.

**References:**
- [Sturrock (1971)](<https://doi.org/10.1086/150865>)

## Spin-up line

**Year:** 1972
**Topic:** Population and evolution
**Type:**

Approximate spin-equilibrium locus for an accreting magnetized neutron star, commonly evaluated near the Eddington accretion rate. Its position depends on magnetic field, accretion rate, stellar parameters, magnetospheric coupling and torque prescription, so it is not a universal minimum spin period.

**References:**
- [Pringle &amp; Rees (1972)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1972A%26A....21....1P/abstract>)

## Davies et al. Jodrell Bank survey

**Year:** 1973
**Topic:** Population and evolution
**Type:** Survey

The Jodrell Bank survey discovered 39 pulsars and used the expanded sample to study their Galactic distribution. It helped turn pulsar searches from individual discovery programmes into quantitative population mapping.

**References:**
- [Davies et al. (1973)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1977MNRAS.179..635D/abstract>)

## Low-frequency spectral turnovers in pulsars

**Year:** 1973
**Topic:** Emission and magnetospheric physics
**Type:**

Broadband measurements showed that steep pulsar radio spectra can flatten or turn over toward low frequencies instead of following one power law. Such departures constrain intrinsic emission and propagation or absorption while informing low-frequency survey design.

**References:**
- [Sieber (1973)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1973A%26A....28..237S/abstract>)

## LAE (linear acceleration emission)

**Year:** 1973, 1978
**Topic:** Coherent radiation
**Type:**

Charges accelerated parallel to the magnetic field radiate without transverse gyration. Cocke proposed stimulated LAE as a pulsar radio mechanism, and Melrose developed the amplified maser theory and its spectral consequences.

**References:**
- [Cocke (1973)](<https://doi.org/10.1086/152326>)
- [Melrose (1978)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1978ApJ...225..557M/abstract>)

## OPMs (orthogonal polarization modes)

**Year:** 1974, 1975, 1976
**Topic:** Polarisation and magnetic geometry
**Type:**

Single pulses often switch between two polarization states whose linear PAs (position angles) differ by approximately 90 degrees. Their superposition explains abrupt PA jumps and depolarization, while linking observed profiles to natural propagation modes in the magnetospheric plasma.

**References:**
- [Rankin et al. (1974)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1974ApJ...188..609R/abstract>)
- [Manchester et al. (1975)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1975ApJ...196...83M/abstract>)
- [Backer et al. (1976)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1976Natur.263..202B/abstract>)

## Causal upper mass limit for neutron stars

**Year:** 1974
**Topic:** Neutron star structure and fundamental physics
**Type:**

Assuming GR (general relativity), microscopic stability and causality, and an EOS (equation of state) known below a fiducial matching density, Rhoades and Ruffini proved that the largest mass is obtained by adopting the maximally stiff causal EOS at higher densities. They derived an absolute upper limit of approximately 3.2 <i>M</i><sub>&#9737;</sub>, establishing a robust mass ceiling for distinguishing neutron stars from black holes.

**References:**
- [Rhoades &amp; Ruffini (1974)](<https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.32.324>)

## First binary pulsar discovered

**Year:** 1974, 1975, 1993
**Topic:** Timing and dynamics
**Type:** Discovery

Joseph Taylor and Russell Hulse discovered PSR B1913+16, the first binary pulsar. Its orbital decay provided indirect evidence for GWs (gravitational waves), and the discovery led to the 1993 Nobel Prize in Physics.

**References:**
- [Hulse &amp; Taylor (1975)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1975ApJ...195L..51H/abstract>)
- [Nobel Prize (1993)](<https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1993/summary/>)

## Thin-screen and distributed-medium pulse broadening

**Year:** 1974
**Topic:** Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
**Type:**

Multipath propagation gives rays different geometric delays and broadens an impulse into a PBF (pulse-broadening function). Williamson derived distinct response functions for a localized thin screen and for scattering distributed along a substantial part of the path, establishing that an exponential tail is geometry-dependent rather than universal.

**References:**
- [Williamson (1974)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1974MNRAS.166..499W/abstract>)

## Anderson-Itoh model of pulsar glitches

**Year:** 1975
**Topic:** Neutron star structure and fundamental physics
**Type:**

Anderson and Itoh proposed that pulsar glitches and rotational irregularities arise from the irregular motion of quantized vortices through the neutron superfluid in the stellar crust. Their work established superfluid vortex dynamics as the foundation of modern glitch theory, including models involving vortex pinning, collective unpinning, and angular-momentum transfer to the crust.

**References:**
- [Anderson &amp; Itoh (1975)](<https://doi.org/10.1038/256025a0>)

## Hulse-Taylor Arecibo survey

**Year:** 1975
**Topic:** Population and evolution
**Type:** Survey

A deep Arecibo search discovered 40 pulsars and probed their spatial extent through the Galactic disk. Its discoveries included PSR B1913+16 and demonstrated the scientific return of high-sensitivity targeted pulsar surveys.

**References:**
- [Hulse &amp; Taylor (1975)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1975ApJ...201L..55H/abstract>)

## Ruderman-Sutherland polar gap model

**Year:** 1975
**Topic:** Inner acceleration
**Type:**

The Ruderman-Sutherland model introduced an inner polar gap above the neutron-star magnetic pole, where intense electric fields accelerate particles to ultra-relativistic speeds; it became a foundational framework for coherent radio emission and drifting subpulses.

**References:**
- [Ruderman &amp; Sutherland (1975)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1975ApJ...196...51R/abstract>)

## Coherent dedispersion

**Year:** 1975
**Topic:**
**Type:** Instrumentation

Applying the inverse cold-plasma transfer function to complex voltage data removes dispersive phase delays before detection. Unlike incoherent channel shifting, it eliminates intra-channel smearing and preserves the time resolution needed for millisecond pulsars and precision timing.

**References:**
- [Hankins &amp; Rickett (1975)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1975mcpr...14...55H/abstract>)

## Blandford-Teukolsky binary timing model

**Year:** 1976
**Topic:** Timing and dynamics
**Type:**

The first widely used binary-pulsar timing formula combined Keplerian orbital motion with propagation, gravitational-redshift and relativistic-delay terms. It established the practical framework from which later high-precision post-Keplerian timing models developed.

**References:**
- [Blandford &amp; Teukolsky (1976)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1976ApJ...205..580B/abstract>)

## Interpulse alternative hypothesis

**Year:** 1977
**Topic:** Beam geometry
**Type:**

Manchester and Lyne proposed that interpulses can represent emission from the extreme edges of a single wide beam.

**References:**
- [Manchester &amp; Lyne (1977)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1977MNRAS.181..761M/abstract>)

## Interstellar scattering and scintillation theory

**Year:** 1977
**Topic:** Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
**Type:**

Rickett synthesized pulse broadening, angular broadening and intensity scintillation from electron-density fluctuations. The transition occurs near <span class="math-symbol">r<sub>diff</sub> ~ r<sub>F</sub></span>: scattering is weak for <span class="math-symbol">r<sub>diff</sub> &#8805; r<sub>F</sub></span> and strong for <span class="math-symbol">r<sub>diff</sub> &#8810; r<sub>F</sub></span>, where <span class="math-symbol">r<sub>F</sub></span> is the Fresnel scale.

**References:**
- [Rickett (1977)](<https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.aa.15.090177.002403>)

## Second Molonglo pulsar survey

**Year:** 1978
**Topic:** Population and evolution
**Type:** Survey

The second Molonglo survey extended systematic southern-sky pulsar searching with improved coverage and sensitivity. Its enlarged sample supported studies of the Galactic pulsar distribution, luminosity function and survey selection effects.

**References:**
- [Manchester et al. (1978)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1978MNRAS.185..409M/abstract>)

## SCLF (space-charge-limited flow) polar-cap model

**Year:** 1979
**Topic:** Inner acceleration
**Type:**

The SCLF polar-cap model assumes charges freely escape the stellar surface; acceleration occurs because the relativistic plasma flow cannot adjust its charge density quickly enough to match the changing geometry of curved magnetic field lines.

**References:**
- [Arons &amp; Scharlemann (1979)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1979ApJ...231..854A/abstract>)

## ADE (anomalous Doppler emission) and the cyclotron-Cherenkov maser

**Year:** 1979, 1991, 1999
**Topic:** Coherent radiation
**Type:**

Early cyclotron-instability work developed into a direct radio-emission model in which anomalous cyclotron-Cherenkov and Cherenkov-drift resonances amplify escaping electromagnetic modes in the outer magnetosphere.

**References:**
- [Machabeli &amp; Usov (1979)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1979SvAL....5..238M/abstract>)
- [Kazbegi et al. (1991)](<https://doi.org/10.1071/PH910573>)
- [Lyutikov, Blandford &amp; Machabeli (1999)](<https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-8711.1999.02443.x>)

## Density power spectrum in the local interstellar medium

**Year:** 1981
**Topic:** Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
**Type:**

Scintillation measurements of 17 pulsars were used to constrain the small-scale spatial spectrum of interstellar electron-density irregularities. Comparison with thin-screen models favoured steep power-law spectra and provided quantitative limits on fluctuation power at scales sampled by pulsar scintillation.

**References:**
- [Armstrong et al. (1981)](<https://doi.org/10.1038/291561a0>)

## Recycling model

**Year:** 1982, 1991, 2002, 2013
**Topic:** Population and evolution
**Type:**

Mass and angular momentum transferred from a binary companion spin an old neutron star up to millisecond periods while reducing its observable magnetic field. The model explains MSPs (millisecond pulsars), their frequent white-dwarf companions and their connection to low-mass X-ray binaries.

**References:**
- [Alpar et al. (1982)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1982Natur.300..728A/abstract>)
- [Radhakrishnan &amp; Srinivasan (1982)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1982CSci...51.1096R/abstract>)
- [Bhattacharya &amp; van den Heuvel (1991)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1991PhR...203....1B/abstract>)
- [Podsiadlowski et al. (2002)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ApJ...565.1107P/abstract>)
- [Papitto et al. (2013)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013Natur.501..517P/abstract>)

## First MSP (millisecond pulsar) discovered

**Year:** 1982
**Topic:** Population and evolution
**Type:** Discovery

PSR B1937+21 rotates once every approximately 1.56 milliseconds, far faster than the pulsars then known. Its discovery revealed the recycled millisecond-pulsar population and motivated binary-accretion spin-up as its evolutionary origin.

**References:**
- [Backer et al. (1982)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1982Natur.300..615B/abstract>)

## Electromagnetic cascades in pulsars

**Year:** 1982
**Topic:** Inner acceleration
**Type:**

Primary particles accelerated above a polar cap emit gamma rays that convert into electron-positron pairs in the strong magnetic field, producing successive cascade generations. These cascades supply magnetospheric plasma and connect gap electrodynamics to radio and high-energy emission.

**References:**
- [Daugherty &amp; Harding (1982)](<https://doi.org/10.1086/159561>)

## Scintillation velocities and screen kinematics

**Year:** 1982, 1998
**Topic:** Interstellar Medium and Galaxy
**Type:**

<b>Observation:</b> Diffractive scintillation characteristics were shown to correlate with independently measured pulsar proper motions. <b>Later significance:</b> The scintillation speed inferred from decorrelation bandwidth and timescale became a geometry-dependent effective velocity combining pulsar, observer and plasma motions, rather than a direct pulsar-velocity measurement.

**References:**
- [Lyne &amp; Smith (1982)](<https://doi.org/10.1038/298825a0>)
- [Cordes &amp; Rickett (1998)](<https://doi.org/10.1086/306358>)

## Hellings-Downs curve

**Year:** 1983
**Topic:** Timing and dynamics
**Type:**

An isotropic stochastic gravitational-wave background produces a distinctive quadrupolar correlation between timing residuals as a function of pulsar angular separation. This angular curve is the principal spatial signature used to distinguish a gravitational-wave background from clock, ephemeris and pulsar-specific noise.

**References:**
- [Hellings &amp; Downs (1983)](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1983ApJ...265L..39H/abstract>)

## Galactic electron-density structure from pulsar sightlines intersecting H II regions

**Year:** 2024

**Type:**
